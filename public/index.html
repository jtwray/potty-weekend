Our little potty game

“When your body says pee or poop, we go to the potty. I’ll help you.”

  1. Notice & tell. “You listened to your body!”
  2. Go & get ready. “You found the potty.” Pants down when wearing them.
  3. Sit comfortably. Feet supported. “Let your tummy be soft.”
  4. A short, relaxed try. “Thanks for trying!” Get up if nothing comes or your child wants to stop.
  5. Wipe, dress & wash. “Soap bubbles, then back to play!”

Warm, specific praise beats a big performance. Sounds are off by default.

Parent guide · the gist in a minute

A calm weekend at home

Start when your child shows readiness and willingness. Keep the potty nearby, supervise, and use easy-clean play areas. Bare-bottom time is optional, private, and only while awake; use a diaper for sleep if needed. Offer usual drinks—no forced extra fluids.

Day 1: Notice cues and learn the route. Day 2: Repeat the same routine. Day 3: Try easy clothing if your child is comfortable. Adjust to the child; the weekend starts learning.

Offer a check-in after waking, around usual poop times or after meals, before leaving, and when you see holding, wiggling, or hiding. The timer supports you; it does not overrule your child.

No pressure to produce

Praise communicating, walking over, clothing steps, sitting, trying, and washing. You don’t need to score every step. For an accident: “Pee goes in the potty. Let’s get comfy and clean.” Never shame or force a sit.

A silly gentle moo or an easy breath can make a short try playful if your child likes it. It is not a proven technique to make pee happen. Support feet, let the belly relax, and avoid pushing, straining, or breath-holding.

Keep handwashing after toileting, including practice sits. Help with the steps and make it brief and familiar. If your child is distressed, comfort them and pause practice.

Optional bowl calibration

With a clean removable bowl on a level surface, add water in measured increments (for example, 25 mL at a time), recording the cumulative amount. A curved bowl needs measured-water calibration, not equally spaced height lines. Use manufacturer-approved markings; check before using permanent ink inside the bowl. Existing molded marks or marks on the outside of a transparent bowl are easier to clean. Empty and clean before use. Never slow down helping your child to measure or photograph.

When to pause

Readiness varies. If fear or resistance builds, take a break. Painful urination, constipation, or withholding deserves a pediatrician’s advice.
